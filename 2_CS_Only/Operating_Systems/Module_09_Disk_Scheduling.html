💿 Module 9: Disk Scheduling

Seek Time, Latency, SCAN, LOOK, and SSTF Algorithms

📖 Core Fundamental Theory: Disk Anatomy

A magnetic hard disk (HDD) is not a solid block of memory. It consists of spinning magnetic platters and a mechanical arm that moves back and forth to read data. Because moving a mechanical arm is incredibly slow (compared to CPU speeds), the OS must optimize exactly how the arm moves to serve multiple read/write requests.

1. Seek Time

The time taken by the mechanical arm to move to the correct Track (or Cylinder). This is the slowest part of disk access!

2. Rotational Latency

The time taken for the spinning platter to rotate the correct Sector directly under the arm's head.

3. Transfer Time

The time taken to actually read/write the bits off the magnetic surface and send them to RAM.

Total Disk Access Time = Seek Time + Rotational Latency + Transfer Time

2️⃣ The 6 Disk Scheduling Algorithms

In GATE, you will be given a queue of requested tracks (e.g., 98, 183, 37, 122, 14, 124, 65, 67) and the current position of the Read/Write Head. You must calculate the Total Head Movement (Total tracks crossed).

1. FCFS (First Come First Serve)

Moves the head exactly in the order the requests arrived.

Verdict: Fair, but terrible performance. Causes wild back-and-forth arm movements.

2. SSTF (Shortest Seek Time First)

Like SJF in CPU scheduling, it picks the request that is mathematically closest to the current head position.

Verdict: Great performance, but causes Starvation (distant tracks might never get serviced if close ones keep arriving).

3. SCAN (The Elevator Algorithm)

The head moves continuously in one direction, servicing requests, until it hits the absolute physical end of the disk (e.g., track 199 or 0). Then it reverses direction.

4. C-SCAN (Circular SCAN)

Moves to the absolute end of the disk servicing requests. But when it hits the end, it immediately returns to the absolute beginning (track 0) without servicing any requests on the return trip. Then it scans again.

Verdict: Provides a much more uniform waiting time than SCAN.

5. LOOK

A smarter version of SCAN. It moves in one direction until the last request in that direction. It does NOT go to the physical end of the disk (track 199) unless there is actually a request there.

6. C-LOOK (Circular LOOK)

The smarter version of C-SCAN. Jumps to the farthest request (not track 0) and scans again.

3️⃣ GATE Traps to Watch Out For

⚠️ TRAP 1: Direction is Everything in SCAN/LOOK

If the question says "Head is currently at 50" and asks you to use SCAN, you cannot solve the problem unless the question specifies the initial direction ("moving towards 0" or "moving towards 199"). If they don't specify, you must calculate both and look at the options.

⚠️ TRAP 2: Bouncing off the wall in SCAN

Remember: SCAN bounces off the physical wall (track 0 or track N-1). LOOK bounces off the lowest/highest requested track. In SCAN, you MUST include the distance from the last request to the wall in your total head movement math!
